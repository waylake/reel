Documentation

When something does not work.

Reel is a front end for yt-dlp, and yt-dlp is in a permanent race with the sites it reads. Most failures are upstream changes, not app bugs. This page is ordered the way support should be: cheapest fix first.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 Sonoma or later.
  • Apple silicon or Intel. The app and its bundled binaries are universal.
  • About 60 MB for the download; the installed app is larger because it carries yt-dlp and ffmpeg.

First launch

Reel is signed ad-hoc, not notarized by Apple. macOS will refuse to open it the first time, with a dialog that says the developer cannot be verified.

  1. Move Reel.app to /Applications.
  2. Right-click the app and choose Open. Confirm once.
  3. After that it launches normally, including after updates.

If you installed with Homebrew, there is no prompt — the cask clears the quarantine attribute after install.

A download failed

Work down this list. In practice, step 1 resolves most failures.

  1. Update the engine. Settings → Advanced → yt-dlp → Update. The new binary takes effect immediately; there is no restart.
  2. Retry. Reel retries transient HTTP errors with exponential backoff on its own, but a manual retry after an update is often what clears a stale URL.
  3. Check the same URL in Terminal with yt-dlp -v "URL". If it fails there too, the problem is upstream and no setting in Reel will fix it.
  4. Cookies. Age-restricted, members-only or private content needs a signed-in session. See Cookies.
  5. Report it. Include the exact error line and the yt-dlp version shown in Settings. Open an issue.

Error messages

MessageWhat it meansWhat to do
HTTP Error 403: Forbidden YouTube refused the request, usually a stale signature or a changed client. Update the engine, then retry.
The page needs to be reloaded Bot detection tripped; it can appear and clear on its own. Retry, then update. Reel does both automatically.
Sign in to confirm you're not a bot An IP-level judgement. Retrying will not clear it. Try cookies from a signed-in browser, a different network, or wait it out.
This video is age-restricted The account in the cookies cannot play it. Pass cookies from a browser that is signed in and can play the video.
Members-only / Private video Requires the account that has access. Cookies from that account.
429, 502, 503, timeouts Transient network or rate limiting. Nothing. Reel backs off and retries; lower concurrency in Settings if it persists.

The engine

Reel bundles two binaries and never implements extraction itself:

  • yt-dlp — reads the page, negotiates formats, downloads. Unlicense.
  • ffmpeg — merges separate video and audio streams, converts audio, writes metadata and subtitles.

The bundled copy is a fallback. If you update yt-dlp from Settings, Reel keeps the newer binary in ~/Library/Application Support/Reel/bin/yt-dlp and prefers it over the one inside the app. That way an app update never silently rolls the engine back.

Reel does not pass its own --extractor-args client overrides. The yt-dlp defaults are maintained by the people who track YouTube every day, and hand-picked clients are the most common cause of avoidable breakage.

Cookies

For content that needs a signed-in session, choose a browser in Settings → Downloads → Login cookies. Reel reads the cookie store directly on your Mac and hands the cookies to yt-dlp for that request only. They are not uploaded, cached or logged.

Safari, Chrome and Firefox are supported. If the cookie export fails — for example a locked keychain or a browser that changed its storage format — Reel falls back to a normal signed-out download instead of failing the item.

Updates

Reel updates itself with Sparkle: the app reads a signed appcast, downloads a signed archive, and installs it on relaunch. There is no account and no update server of ours — the feed is a static file on GitHub Releases.

You can turn automatic checks off in Settings. Manual: Reel → Check for Updates.

Uninstall

rm -rf /Applications/Reel.app
rm -rf ~/Library/Application\ Support/Reel
defaults delete com.waylake.reel

Your downloaded files are not touched. With Homebrew: brew uninstall --cask reel and brew zap --cask reel if you also want the support files gone.

Legal

Reel is a local tool. It does not host, index or distribute media, and it does not circumvent DRM. Whether a download is lawful depends on the content, the site's terms and where you live. Download what you own, what is offered under a licence that permits it, or what your local law allows. You are responsible for what you save.

Issues with a specific site are usually fixed upstream in yt-dlp before they are fixed in Reel. Checking yt-dlp's issue tracker first saves everyone time.
